PLI at the halfway mark: ₹2.40 lakh crore in, 28% of the jobs

NITI Aayog, with IIM Ahmedabad as knowledge partner, has released a mid-course Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes (12 November 2026) covering all 14 Production Linked Incentive sectors.

WHAT IT MEASURES
Each sector on four outcomes — investment, production, exports and employment — read against 50% of full-tenure targets. The implementation period is 58% complete.

THE SCORECARD (to March 2026)
• Investment: ₹2,40,138 crore — 76% of the ₹3.15 lakh crore target
• Production and sales: ₹22,66,412 crore — 64% of target
• Employment: 8,49,069 jobs — only 28% of a 30.19 lakh target
• Incentives disbursed: ₹35,354 crore, 18.51% of the ₹1.91 lakh crore outlay

WHAT WORKED
Mobile phone exports crossed ₹2 lakh crore in FY2025 for the first time. Bulk drugs turned a ₹1,930 crore trade deficit (FY2021-22) into a ₹2,280 crore surplus (FY2024-25). Pharma exports rose 32.7%. Drone sector turnover grew seven-fold.

WHAT LAGS
Employment, domestic value addition and disbursement. Technical textiles has used just 3.61% of its funds; ACC batteries is held back by localisation norms, long gestation and critical-mineral dependence.

WAY FORWARD
Sector-specific design rather than one template, demand-pull measures alongside supply incentives, deeper MSME participation, harmonised data systems.

UPSC relevance: GS-3 (industrial policy, manufacturing, employment) and Prelims (scheme architecture, administering bodies, sector coverage).

Background reading on how manufacturing is shifting globally: https://lotusarise.com/global-shift-in-manufacturing-upsc/

If the incentives delivered investment but not employment, is the problem the scheme's design or the capital-intensity of the sectors it chose?

NITI Aayog has released its mid-course Impact Assessment of PLI Schemes, prepared with IIM Ahmedabad and covering all 14 Production Linked Incentive sectors.

The headline numbers, cumulative to March 2026:

Investment realised: ₹2,40,138 crore — 76% of the full-tenure target
Production and sales: ₹22,66,412 crore — 64% of target
Employment: 8,49,069 jobs — 28% of a 30.19 lakh target
Incentives disbursed: ₹35,354 crore, 18.51% of the ₹1,91,049 crore outlay

With 58% of the implementation period elapsed, investment and output are comfortably ahead of a 50% mid-course benchmark. Employment is not.

That asymmetry is the finding worth sitting with. The sectors that absorbed the most capital — large-scale electronics, pharmaceuticals, solar modules — are capital-intensive by design. Technical textiles, a more labour-intensive segment, has drawn just 3.61% of its allocation despite exports rising 59% and imports falling 49.4% between 2021 and 2025; the report attributes the gap to high eligibility thresholds and rigid HSN classification.

The successes are real. Mobile phone exports crossed ₹2 lakh crore in FY2025. Bulk drugs moved from a ₹1,930 crore trade deficit to a ₹2,280 crore surplus. Drone sector turnover grew seven-fold.

NITI Aayog's recommendations are correspondingly structural: sector-specific design instead of a single template, demand-pull measures alongside supply-side incentives, deeper MSME participation, and harmonised data systems across implementing ministries.

For UPSC aspirants this is now the reference case study for GS-3 on industrial policy — and a reminder that capacity creation and employment generation are separate policy problems.

### The question
Consider the following statements regarding the Production Linked Incentive (PLI) schemes:

1. The PLI schemes were launched in 2020 and currently cover 14 sectors.
2. PLI incentives are computed on incremental sales over a base year and are released only after prescribed investment and production conditions are met.
3. The 2026 impact assessment of the PLI schemes was prepared by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry with the World Bank as knowledge partner.
4. By March 2026 the schemes had met a larger share of their employment target than of their investment target.

Which of the statements given above is/are correct?
(A) 1 and 2 only
(B) 2 and 3 only
(C) 1, 3 and 4 only
(D) 1, 2 and 4 only

### The explanation
Statement 1 — Correct. PLI began in 2020 as part of the Atmanirbhar Bharat package and covers 14 notified sectors.
Statement 2 — Correct. Incentives are calculated on incremental sales over a base year and are paid only after the prescribed investment and production thresholds are met, which is why disbursement (₹35,354 crore, 18.51% of outlay) trails approvals.
Statement 3 — Incorrect. The assessment was prepared by NITI Aayog with IIM Ahmedabad as knowledge partner, not by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry with the World Bank.
Statement 4 — Incorrect, and reversed. Investment stood at 76% of its full-tenure target; employment at only 28%.

Global Carbon Budget 2026: the world dips, India rises

The Global Carbon Project's 21st annual carbon budget landed during COP31 at Antalya, Türkiye — a preprint in Earth System Science Data on 10 November 2026, built by more than 140 scientists across 90-plus institutions.

THE GLOBAL PICTURE
• Fossil CO₂: 38.2 billion tonnes in 2026, down 0.4%
• Land-use change CO₂: 5.1 billion tonnes, and rising — driven by peat fires and forest degradation in Southeast Asia
• Total CO₂: 43.3 billion tonnes, up from 42.8 billion tonnes in 2025
• Atmospheric CO₂: 427.7 ppm, about 54% above pre-industrial levels
• Remaining budget for 1.5 °C: 150 billion tonnes — under four years at current rates

BY FUEL AND BY COUNTRY
Oil is down 1.9% and gas 0.3%, but coal is up 0.6%. China is down 0.9%, the United States 1.3%, the European Union 1.5%, Japan 1%. India is projected up 4.4% — the one large economy rising.

WHY INDIA IS RISING
A drier monsoon and a hotter year pushed cooling demand up; coal still dominates power generation; vehicle ownership and road travel keep climbing; and steel, cement and construction keep fossil demand growing. Renewable capacity is expanding, but not yet fast enough to offset demand growth.

Note the uncomfortable arithmetic: fossil emissions fell, yet total emissions rose, because land-use change went the other way.

UPSC relevance: GS-3 (climate change, energy transition) and Prelims (ppm figures, the 1.5 °C budget, the publishing body, COP31's venue).
